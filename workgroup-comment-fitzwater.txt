Submitted to the House Education Committee Workgroup on School Governance
Re: Proposed Enrollment Attendance Zone / School Choice Policies Language, Draft 1.3
March 15, 2026

My name is Cait Fitzwater. I am a parent and resident of Waterford, Vermont, and a member of OurKidsOurSchoolsVT, a volunteer coalition of Vermont parents and community members concerned about the impact of current education legislation on rural towns that depend on independent schools.

I am writing to raise a specific concern about Draft 1.3 that I believe has not been fully addressed in the current language.

WATERFORD AND PEACHAM ARE NOT PROTECTED BY SECTION A(2)

Section A(2) of the proposed language provides an exemption for students in districts that did not operate a school for a given grade band, allowing those students to apply to any public or approved independent school rather than being assigned a default district school.

Waterford and Peacham are both member towns of the Caledonia Central Supervisory Union. Waterford operates a K-8 school. Peacham operates a K-6 school. Neither town operates a high school. However, Caledonia Central Supervisory Union does operate high schools within the district.

Under the plain reading of Section A(2), Waterford and Peacham students would not qualify for the exemption because the district — not the individual town — operates schools in the applicable grade band. These students would instead be assigned a default district high school under the attendance zone policy.

This directly conflicts with Section A(1), which states the policy shall align to attendance patterns as they exist on July 1, 2026. Waterford and Peacham high school students have a long-established pattern of attending St. Johnsbury Academy through town tuitioning. That pattern would be effectively overridden by the default assignment mechanism in Section A(2), even though Section A(1) is intended to preserve it.

THE CONFLICT BETWEEN SECTION A(1) AND SECTION A(2)

As currently drafted, Section A(1) and Section A(2) pull in opposite directions for towns like Waterford and Peacham. Section A(1) promises to preserve existing attendance patterns. Section A(2) creates a default assignment mechanism that, for member towns of districts that operate high schools, overrides those patterns without providing a clear pathway back to the independent schools families have relied on for generations.

This is not a hypothetical concern. It is a specific, foreseeable outcome for real families in named towns under the current draft language.

REQUESTED CLARIFICATION

I respectfully request that the workgroup clarify whether the "established patterns" protection in Section A(1) is intended to supersede the default assignment in Section A(2) where those patterns include attendance at approved independent schools. If not, I ask that the language be amended to explicitly protect member towns where no local high school exists, regardless of whether another school in the district serves that grade band.

Without this clarification or amendment, the draft as written would eliminate the independent school pathway for Waterford and Peacham families — not through an explicit policy decision, but through a gap in the language that I do not believe was intended.

Thank you for the opportunity to comment.

Cait Fitzwater
Waterford, Vermont
